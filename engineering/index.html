Five principles

How we build.

The same five engineering principles run through every aircraft in the family, from the 1-11NXT's T-tail to the 4-44ULR's auxiliary fuel system.

01

Composite-first construction

Over 72% of structural airframe weight is advanced carbon-fibre composite: one-piece fuselage barrels, co-cured wing skins and a composite empennage. Lighter structure, no corrosion, and fatigue lives measured in decades rather than cycles.

72%Composite by weight
−18%Fuselage mass vs aluminium
02

Next-generation propulsion

Ultra-high-bypass turbofans on every variant, built in Derby. Lean-burn combustors cut NOx, acoustic liners tame the fan, and every engine is certified for 100% sustainable aviation fuel — with hydrogen-hybrid readiness designed into the pylon and fuel architecture.

03

One flight deck philosophy

Side-stick fly-by-wire with full envelope protection across the family. Common cockpit layouts mean the 1-11NXT and 2-22 share a training pipeline, and the 3-33 and 4-44 share a single type rating.

04

Cabins for people

Higher humidity and a lower cabin altitude reduce fatigue on long sectors. Panoramic windows, circadian lighting and quieter engines make a 20-hour flight feel like a fraction of that.

British manufacturing

Final assembly at Bristol Filton. Wings from Broughton. Engines from Derby. Composite fuselage barrels from Belfast. Every major component sourced from British soil, with suppliers across all four nations.

Sustainability roadmap

Designed for the long runway.

Aircraft we deliver in 2030 will still be flying in 2060. We design them to get cleaner over their lives, not just at delivery.

  1. 2028

    SAF-100 at entry into service

    The 1-11NXT enters service certified for 100% sustainable aviation fuel — no blending limits.

  2. 2032

    Hydrogen-hybrid demonstrator

    A 4-44 testbed flies with a hydrogen-electric hybrid powering one engine position.

  3. 2035

    Engines-off taxi

    Electric taxi and APU-free turnarounds become standard across the family.

  4. 2040

    Net-zero manufacturing

    Every BAC site powered by renewable energy, with closed-loop recycling of composite offcuts.
